**Design version 1.0, 14 October 2026**  
**Project:** Pokémon Averon — Emerald Expansion, second region Sinnoh

## Non-negotiable tone
A traditional Pokémon adventure comes first. Exploration, humor, catching, friendships, varied cultures, regional lore, and battle challenges occupy the overwhelming majority of play time. Only a minority of conversations touch the mystery; only some important characters are conspirators. Do **not** make every scene ominous or every NPC a clue. Mature stakes are reserved for well-spaced pivotal events, especially after Gym 6. Avoid speeches, edgy behavior, gratuitous violence, and melodramatic deaths. NPCs have independent everyday concerns. Scenes should be paced as playable activities, not dialogue marathons.

## The three layers of Threshold — separate them at all times
**1. Government intention:** Project Threshold temporarily neutralized an exceptionally dangerous Pokémon by extracting its extraordinary energy. A filtration stage was intended to make that extracted power safe and to restore it afterward. The government/League had a credible public-safety reason for researching and then securing the apparatus.

**2. Team Vantage's public mission:** **End humanity's dependence on Pokémon.** Build non-Pokémon infrastructure and systems so a town doesn't require a talented trainer to function safely. This mission is genuinely supported by many people, some of the team's lower ranks, and—eventually—the childhood rival. The group initially behaves like a fairly ordinary crime syndicate; the public cause becomes more relevant after its coup. This is NOT a program to remove Pokémon, seize all Pokémon, or ban companionship.

**3. Leader's secret aim:** Weaponize what happened in the original Threshold catastrophe into a large-area Pokémon-targeting annihilation pulse. The boss witnessed a malfunction: extracted energy accumulated; filtration failed; a dimensional breach opened; a highly concentrated feedback discharge killed or seemingly erased the Pokémon test subject; Zygarde emerged in the aftermath. The boss believes the particular destructive pulse can be engineered and amplified, using the government-held device, classified blueprints and state infrastructure. He knows people and Pokémon are at risk from dimensional collapse and chooses to proceed. The purpose of the coup is obtaining *unrestricted control* of Threshold, not merely conducting reforms.

**Tech/lore consistency for writers:** A Pokémon's death resulted from **concentrated feedback at the machine**, after being drained and exposed to an abnormal energy phase; normal ambient conditions in the other dimension do not necessarily produce the same effect. The villain weapon would deliberately run the two-stage extraction / destructive feedback in reverse-engineered sequence. Human exposure to the first experiment did not produce the same instantaneous Pokémon-specific biological effect, but spatial instability was dangerous to humans. Zygarde likely responded to the disturbed boundary and survived passage because the lethal feedback burst was localized and temporally separate; its cell-based resilience can be examined without asserting definitive canon biology. In the finale, damage alters the machine's geometry: instead of releasing the focused pulse, it opens a large gravitational/spatial rupture; the player and antagonist are pulled inside. Exact explanatory language in game should be minimal and grounded in visible evidence. Scientists can describe a hypothesis; don't pretend it is an established law of real physics.

## Protagonist and starter opening
The player lives in **Saltmere** with a friendly hometown circle and visits three ordinary places before receiving a starter. The first meaningful household interaction—not an explicit starter prompt—determines which of **Snivy (greenhouse), Fennekin (workshop), Piplup (harbor)** is later offered. The opening does not announce this mechanism. Provide subtle consistency and a confirmation point to avoid a bad surprise. The childhood friend receives the starter with a normal type advantage relative to the player's starter; the easygoing rival receives the third. The opening includes small errands, a short beach view, a first wild encounter, and a shared promise to visit the League. Don't over-script it.

## Recurring cast — design names are provisional, roles are binding
- **Renn**, childhood friend/main rival. Friendly, talented, sometimes quietly sure he is better than the player. Keep that trait subtle. His repeated setbacks at Slateford Gym are engineered by Gym Leader Dorian, who uses illegal counterteams for Renn. Renn grows impatient with his Pokémon, gradually shifts ambition from becoming Champion to creating a society that does not depend on Pokémon. A Team Vantage mentor offers practical opportunities; he joins and later battles the player in an unwinnable story encounter. The player and allies uncover the rigging **before** the final act; Renn has time to understand it, and the manipulation doesn't excuse the choices he subsequently makes. The final disposition should be consequential but understated: he helps avert the erasure operation after learning the full truth, takes responsibility for his own actions, and returns in Sinnoh as a quiet but still formidable independent trainer. Do not reset friendship instantly.
- **Mira**, second rival. Curious, playful, enjoys unconventional Pokémon; travels for discovery rather than badges. Helps with an optional ecology quest and later one part of the final assault. Never turns evil.
- **Detective Nessa Calder**, investigator. Good at witnesses, law-enforcement patterns, and mobilizing aid; frequently revises hypotheses. The player finds decisive evidence through exploration. Calder does not deliver every revelation or narrate every mission.
- **Tavi**, junior Pokémon field researcher. Energetic, occasional comedic mishaps; distributes ecology, migration, form and research quests. Most work has nothing to do with villains.
- **Nell Arlo**, journalist. Reports festivals and Gym battles as often as government affairs. Provides public reaction snapshots through naturally placed small conversations.
- **Orin**, wandering older trainer. Fishing, ruins and remote excursions; optional tough battles and occasional practical advice. He is not secretly Red and must not repeatedly utter mystery lines.
- **Pip**, Saltmere hometown friend pursuing a craft/trade rather than the League. Messages and low-stakes cameos keep the journey anchored in ordinary life.
- **Professor Liora Fenwick**, regional professor. Studies habitat distribution and migrations, supports a broad regional Pokédex. Not a hidden villain.
- **Former Threshold adviser Dr. Hester Quill**, government witness. Can recognize technical schematics; withheld troubling information after the disastrous experiment. A believable mixture of caution, culpability, and help.
- **Director Severin Vale**, leader of Team Vantage. Former Threshold official and firsthand witness to the original accident. Publicly disciplined and persuasive; privately prepared to eliminate Pokémon and accept human collateral damage. Do not give him a cartoonish villain speech. He once had a Pokémon partner he genuinely cared about; his conviction is not shaken by convenient reminders about friendship.
- **Marshal Torren**, criminal security/infrastructure commander with Registeel via a government program; takes it into his own power network.
- **Research director Vela**, commander with Silvally/Type: Null research connections; questions the survival projections but initially remains complicit.
- **Operations commander Sorn**, currently holds Raikou as his long-term partner; separate personal ambition and infrastructure agenda.
- **Field mentor Kade**, the contact who recruits Renn. Their interest in him is both a pragmatic search for competent operators and an attempt to undermine a symbolic young League contender; NOT every villain knows about the bomb.
- **Recurring grunt Kit**, greedy, disorganized, never entrusted with the device's inner workings. Sometimes funny, sometimes genuinely harmful; may be found doing ordinary crime unrelated to the big plot.

## Eight Gym Leaders
1. **Elio — Alderfield — Normal:** warm agricultural community organizer; apple orchard Gym; genuine.
2. **Celia — Breakwater — Flying:** athletic lighthouse keeper; winds and routes; genuine.
3. **Fen — Mossveil — Ghost:** local stage illusionist/conservator; non-ominous, friendly; genuine.
4. **Dorian — Slateford — Fighting:** admired disciplinarian, secretly a Vantage operative. He rigs *Renn's* repeated challenges with illegal teams and uses the player to eliminate commander Aster, his own competing internal rival, while making himself look heroic. His betrayal isn't revealed until later; the commander was a competing careerist, **not** necessarily a whistleblower about Threshold. Upgraded late assault team.
5. **Nadia — Sunscar — Ground:** respected architect whose loyalty is to Vantage's promised construction program and personal influence. After the official Gym victory, locks the doors and issues a *two-Pokémon* illegal no-heal follow-up battle. Losing the ambush never strips the badge, saves money, or softlocks the story. Upgraded late assault team.
6. **Sera — Palehaven — Ice:** alpine safety volunteer. Genuine; joins coalition later.
7. **Kellan — Crownspine — Dragon:** preservationist and mountain guide. Genuine; does not need a secret side.
8. **Mara — Ashfall — Fire:** geothermal engineer and community advocate. Genuine; supports evacuation at finale.

## Main-story beat sheet (paced around playable travel)
The following *anchors* must exist. Most routes, local events, and rival meetings are ordinary Pokémon gameplay, not anchors.

**Prologue, Saltmere:** small home scenes; indirect starter assignment; early friends and Professor Fenwick; first rival battles; cheerful regional festival. No ominous exposition.

**Before Gym 1:** orchard help, open grassland routes, starter dynamics, first badge. Local Pokémon ecology and low-stakes side quests.

**Gyms 1–2:** Breakwater fishing contest; Team Vantage grunts involved in petty theft; separate research document theft from state facility involving **original Threshold blueprints**. Detective Calder interviews the wrong witness first; player helps uncover a missing log. Do not explain blueprints' meaning.

**Gym 3:** forest/mist and independent shrine secrets; Mira and Tavi pursue a habitat survey. Dorian is introduced later as a genuinely helpful public figure. A mundane Vantage warehouse raid offers criminal-team flavor.

**Gym 4 / Slateford:** Renn makes repeated failed attempts against Dorian. Player takes official challenge and wins. In a separate side operation, Dorian helps the player bring down commander Aster, his professional rival; commander recognizes Dorian with a casual pointed remark. Don't reveal collusion. Renn leaves gym circuit after another loss; short, matter-of-fact scene.

**Gym 5 / Sunscar:** desert archaeology and market festival. Nadia gives legitimate badge then uses an illicit two-Pokémon ambush immediately without a heal. Defeat consequences are scripted and forgiving. This is first clear evidence of hidden criminal allegiance in the League. Detective and player jointly pursue records.

**Between Gyms 5–6:** Renn reappears in new role and reveals Vantage allegiance; battle is deliberately unwinnable through robust scripted outcome, not just inflated levels. Fade to Pokémon Center without money loss; no repeated fight or softlock. He has new convictions but little speechifying.

**Gym 6 / mountain rise:** optional lake and lodge adventures continue. Emergency political appointments and changed police behavior become more noticeable; mostly background information. Genuine Sera supports local small events.

**Near Gym 7 — COUP:** a coordinated reveal of embedded Vantage officials; government and League administrative apparatus seized. Vesper changes hands. Show the event through short playable skirmishes and a compact broadcast. It isn't the team's first important victory; it's the endpoint of years of preparation.

**AFTERMATH 1 (first of exactly two mandatory post-coup windows):** return to Vesper and see changed uniforms, posters, reopened transit, brief disagreement among residents; shop/center/gyms otherwise work. No occupation simulation.

**After Gym 7 / departure south:** in player-led exploration, stolen technical plans match original government records. With Calder, Tavi, and Dr. Quill's partial memories, players piece together the original test subject's death, filtration failure, and Zygarde's temporal emergence. A clue is found by PLAYER in a restricted annex rather than delivered by detective.

**AFTERMATH 2:** on southern route / Gullhaven see useful human-built infrastructure, plus concrete costs to individuals; three or fewer concise NPC reactions. No prolonged political debate.

**Gym 8 / Ashfall:** Fire Gym, islands, thermal excursions; interesting unrelated local content and Heatran foreshadowing. Vantage's leadership accelerates Threshold retrofit behind closed doors.

**Truth reveal:** Vantage's PUBLIC promise to end humanity's dependence on Pokémon is distinct from Vale's SECRET attempt to weaponize concentrated feedback energy to erase Pokémon; the coup enabled access to Threshold, classified systems, and state power networks. A minority of elites knew. Renn learns corroborating evidence before finale and has time to make a personal decision. Player/allies also discover evidence Dorian rigged Renn's challenges; no convenient instantaneous heel-face turn.

**Final coalition:** Calder coordinates entry; Mira scouts another approach; Sera and Mara protect public evacuation routes; Tavi handles technical clue; former adviser explains emergency shutdown limitations. The player takes decisive action. Nobody is omniscient.

**Installation gauntlet:** exactly TEN sequential SINGLE-Pokémon hostile operative battles. Distinct teams/strategies and returning faces. A clear pre-gauntlet checkpoint; party persists through all ten; prevent softlock on whiteout, restart with reasonable checkpoint handling.

**Ally heal and Gym boss relay:** a trusted ally heals the player. Battle Dorian and Nadia in succession, now each with upgraded teams and legal Mega mechanics if unlocked. Additional save window only if tested not exploitable. Optionally short rival confrontation before the antagonist, conditional on his arc; avoid gratuitous repeated six-Pokémon battles.

**Initial Vale encounter:** four-Pokémon phase or real midbattle scripted trigger if technically validated. Upon fourth knockout (or post-phase), the player's battle actions and unstable machine break its containment. Instead of the intended wide-area Pokémon-killing pulse, the Threshold geometry tears into a gravitational rupture; player and Vale are drawn to the alternate dimension.

**Dimensional interlude:** short (roughly 15–30 minutes to plan and tune) surreal 2D maze with unfamiliar ecology, orientation/warp puzzles, limited NPC dialogue, no second evil-world story. Party Pokémon survive because ambient dimensional environment isn't the weaponized feedback pulse. Giratina appears as an independent guardian, with no compulsory capture.

**Final Vale battle:** full team after respite / controlled healing as required by difficulty testing. He remains accountable for knowingly risking lives. Defeat destroys his activation opportunity. Giratina leads the way back through a closing rupture; Zygarde's system-scale intervention stabilizes ecological disturbances. The core apparatus can no longer be used for its weaponized purpose.

**Averon League:** eight-badge player challenges Victory Isle's Elite Four and Champion. Fallout is dealt with succinctly, not wall-to-wall dialogue. Continue game normally into Sinnoh.

**Sinnoh second region:** full progression through Sinnoh's towns, routes, Gyms, Mt. Coronet, and League. Major content: familiar landmarks, original side adventures, Cynthia as important recurring character with recognizable personality and Garchomp; proper battle theme only with permission/licensable arrangement. Renn reappears more quietly and battles on even footing late in region. Champions' Circuit is optional/repeatable, not ultimate ending.

**Ultimate optional endgame:** after completing region stories and postgame personal and legendary quests, reach a solitary summit. Two or fewer sparse allusions to a silent, unmatched trainer anywhere beforehand; never identify him. The final trainer is **Red**. No later trainer is narratively stronger and no post-Red story boss supplants him. Levels capped at 100.

## Dialogue limits / style guardrails
- Normal route trainers: usually 1–2 short text boxes before/after battle.
- Recurring nonplot scenes: usually 2–5 boxes.
- Major reveals: preferably 8–15 brief boxes shared among characters with player action/battle interrupting; editing can exceed limit only for clarity.
- Never use prolonged moral lectures. Keep language warm and recognizable as a Pokémon game.
- Main plot clues placed only in selected relevant scenes, not every NPC.
- Every town needs lively unrelated NPCs and optional activities. Only 2 required sequences show post-coup public reaction.
- At least three genuine authority allies remain respected and never flip villain.
- Rival's lines reflect self-confidence very occasionally, no recurring 'I'm better than you' taunts.
